Software-based attacks

Software-based attacks exploit software, networks, or user behavior to access, steal, or damage information. Table 2.7 lists some common software-based attacks along with brief descriptions and examples.

Table 2.7: Lists some common software-based cyber attacks

Phishing

Fake emails or messages that trick you to give personal

info. Example: An email pretending to be your bank

asking for your password.

Malware

Bad software that can damage your device or spy on you.

Example: A fake app that steals your passwords.

Ransomware

Software that locks your files and asks for money to

unlock them. Example: Your computer gets locked and shows a message asking for payment.

Password attacks

Trying to guess or steal your password. Example: Using

common passwords like “1234” to get into your account.

SQL injection

Using bad code to break into websites. Example: Typing secret code in a login box to see private data.

DoS attack

Sending too many fake requests to crash a website.

Example: A website stops working because of too many visitors.

DNS spoofing

Redirecting you to fake websites. Example: You think

you’re on your bank’s site but it’s fake.

Man-in-the-middle

Someone secretly reads or changes your messages.

Example: A hacker spying on your chat on public Wi-Fi.

Salami attack

Stealing small amounts many times without being noticed.

Example: Taking little money from many accounts.

Supply chain attack

Hacking through another company’s software. Example:

A school app gets infected and spreads malware.

Identity theft

Using someone else’s info to pretend to be them. Example:

Making fake social media accounts with stolen details.

Spamming

Sending lots of unwanted emails. Example: Getting many

fake emails about prizes or offers.
