Table 2.11: Steps to conduct computer forensics

Steps

Description

Identification

Detect and identify digital devices and data, such as computers,

smartphones, or storage drives, that may contain evidence of cybercrime.

Preservation

Secure the devices and prevent any changes to the data by

making forensic copies. This ensures the original evidence

remains intact.

Collection

Gather all relevant digital evidence, including files, emails,

browsing history, and deleted data, using specialised forensic tools.

Examination

Analyse the collected data to find important clues, such as signs

of hacking, unauthorised access, or hidden information.

Analysis

Study the evidence carefully to understand how the crime

happened, identify suspects, and determine the impact of the attack.

Documentation

Record all findings, including screenshots, reports, and logs, to

create a detailed forensic report for legal purposes.

Presentation

Present the findings to law enforcement, organisations, or in

court as evidence, explaining how the crime occurred and who

was responsible.

Incident response

and prevention

Provide recommendations on how to improve security and

prevent future cybercrimes based on the investigation results.

Safe browsing

Safe browsing means using the Internet in a way that protects your personal information

and keeps you safe from online threats. To stay safe, avoid clicking on unknown links, do not share personal details like passwords, and use strong passwords for

your accounts. Always visit trusted websites and be careful when downloading files

to avoid viruses. Use antivirus software and keep your device updated to protect it from hackers. If you see something suspicious online, report it to a teacher, parent, or guardian. Safe browsing helps you enjoy the Internet without risks.

Indication of a secure website

(i)

A secure website’s URL begins with “https” rather than “http”. The “s” stands for

“secure” and indicates the site is using a Secure Sockets Layer (SSL) certificate.
