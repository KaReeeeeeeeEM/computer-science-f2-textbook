Catalyst of cybercrime

Cybercrime occurs due to various factors

or motivation, including:

(i)

Financial gain: Most

cybercriminals are motivated by stealing money or data through fraud, ransomware, and phishing.

(ii) Anonymity: The Internet allows

criminals to hide their identity and

operate across borders, making

detection difficult.

(iii) Political and ideological motives:

Some cyberattacks are done to

protest or disrupt governments and

corporations.

(iv) Revenge: Dissatisfied individuals

or competitors may commit

cybercrimes to harm others or gain

an advantage.

(v)

Curiosity and challenge: Some hackers commit crimes for

fun, recognition, or the thrill of

overcoming security systems.

(vi) Weak cybersecurity: Poor

security practices, human error,

and outdated systems create

vulnerabilities.

(vii) Ineffective law enforcement: Weak

laws, jurisdictional challenges, and

underreporting make it difficult to

prosecute cybercriminals.

(viii) Cyber espionage: Governments

may conduct cyberattacks for political, economic, or military

advantage.
