Email security tips

(i)

Create a strong password using

a mix of uppercase, lowercase,

numbers, and symbols. Avoid

common words and birthdays.

(ii)

Enable Two-Factor Authentication

(2FA) to add an extra layer of

security to prevent unauthorised

access.

(iii) Use a password manager to help

manage and store strong passwords

securely.

(iv) Verify the sender by checking

the sender’s email address for

legitimacy before clicking on links.

(v)

Avoid clicking suspicious links;

hover over links to see the actual

URL before clicking.

(vi) Do not download unknown

attachments because malware often

spreads through email attachments.

(vii) Beware of urgent requests because

cybercriminals use urgency to trick

victims into providing sensitive

information.

(viii) Regularly update your password; change your password every few months to reduce security risks.

(ix)

Always sign out when using a

shared or public computer.

(x)

Monitor login activity; many email

providers allow you to check login

history for suspicious access.

(xi)

Use end-to-end encryption to ensure that only the intended

recipient can read your email.

(xii)

Avoid sending confidential

information via email; use secure file-sharing services instead.

(xiii) Do not auto-forward emails. This can expose your email content to unauthorised access.

(xiv) Revoke access to unnecessary apps connected to your email by limiting third-party app access.

(xv)

Regular updates help fix security

vulnerabilities by updating email apps and software.

(xvi) Use a reputable email provider with strong security measures

(such as, Gmail, Outlook,

ProtonMail).

(xvii) Check for fake email addresses

because attackers can slightly alter

addresses to make them look real.

(xviii) Verify requests for money or data; always confirm directly with the sender via another method before acting.
