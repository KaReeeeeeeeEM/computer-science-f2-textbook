Table 2.2: Natural Threats to computer systems

Threat

Description

Natural Disasters (such as, earthquakes, floods, fires, hurricanes, tornadoes, extreme weather)

These unpredictable events can damage hardware through heat, water, or electrical surges, leading to data loss or total equipment failure.

Lightning Strikes

Lightning can cause power surges that may severely

damage or destroy computer hardware, especially if proper surge protection is not in place.

Dust and Humidity

Excessive dust can clog internal cooling systems

and lead to overheating. High humidity may result

in condensation, causing short circuits or corrosion.

Extreme Temperature

Extremely high or low temperatures can affect

computer performance, damage internal components,

or cause systems to shut down unexpectedly.

Magnetic Storms (Solar

Flares)

Solar flares can disrupt satellite communications and

electrical grids, potentially affecting data centers,

servers, and other computer systems.

Manmade threats

Malicious human actors and accidental human errors pose various risks to computer

systems and networks. These threats include intentional actions such as theft, viruses,

spyware, and social engineering, as well as unintentional actions like accidental data deletion and hardware damage. Table 2.3 summarizes different types of man made cyber threats.

Table 2.3: Manmade threats to computer systems

Threat

Description

Theft

Unauthorized individuals can steal your computer,

its parts, or data. The portability of devices like

laptops has increased the risk of physical theft.
